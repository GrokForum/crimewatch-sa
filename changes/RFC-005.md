## POPIA policy (owner rule t704u)

This is the rule for the daily-sweeps page and for any text we write on our own site about a sweep. It replaces earlier draft handle-policy wording.

1. **We may link to external sites that themselves expose personal information.** The exposure is theirs, and we only report, mask and reference.
2. **Every fact needs a reference.** An item without at least one `https` source URL is refused at build time.
3. **On our own page, private people are masked or described anonymously**, for example “a farmer and his wife”. We do not name private victims or suspects, and we do not repeat identifying details (a unique combination of date, town and a unique fact) in our own wording just to make a link more specific. The link itself may still point at the publisher’s page.
4. **This disclaimer appears, worded exactly:** “We are not responsible for anything published by anyone else, including personal information.”
5. **Public figures, officials, police and media accounts can be named.** Their handles may appear in the edition and in the handle search. This includes official SAPS, NPA, government and police accounts, media-organisation accounts, and public-figure or professional media accounts (a journalist’s public professional account, a minister, a commissioner, an MP in their public role).

Consequences for handles:

- A handle is **publishable** only when its role is `official`, `police`, `media` or `public-figure`. Those handles are written in our text and are searchable.
- A **private individual’s** handle is not written on our page and is not in the search index. If a sweep has to mention such a post, our wording stays anonymous (“a resident”, “a farmer and his wife”) and the reference is the external URL, whose exposure is the publisher’s.
- **Anonymous aggregators** and accounts whose official status is unconfirmed are not named or presented as official or media on our page until they are reviewed into one of the publishable roles. A fact they alerted us to may still be published if it has a fetched official or media reference.
- A **protected** account may be named if it belongs to a public figure or professional media person, but we do not quote or link unseen posts. Cite a fetched public byline or official source instead.
- Operational notes, usage figures, local paths, and teammate or agent names never go into the catalog.

The site POPIA and hygiene gate still runs on the generated pages (DRAFT banner, robots, CSP, emails, phones, identity numbers, banned tokens, private-names list, source-register / sweep-citation URL check). PR #3’s tighter entity and format-character scanning should be on `main` before this page is merged, because sweep text will carry more external citations.

The pre-public Hub blocker on farm-incident slugs stands for the Hub. This catalog does not import those rows. Where we later cite a farm incident, our wording stays anonymous and the citation may be the publisher’s URL, per rule 1.

## Data format

One committed file, `data/sweeps.json`, is the catalog. It is reviewed content, not an official-derived pin, and the ingest step must not rewrite it.

```json
{
  "version": 1,
  "disclaimer": "We are not responsible for anything published by anyone else, including personal information.",
  "days": [
    {
      "date": "2026-11-21",
      "compiled_sast": "about 17:25 SAST",
      "summary": "One-line public summary of the edition.",
      "items": [
        {
          "id": "2026-11-21-worcester",
          "kind": "news",
          "title": "Shooting in Worcester",
          "place": "Worcester, Western Cape",
          "summary": "Anonymous wording on our page.",
          "label": "Established",
          "handles": [
            {"handle": "SAPoliceService", "role": "police", "display": "@SAPoliceService"}
          ],
          "sources": [
            {"title": "SAPS official post", "url": "https://x.com/SAPoliceService/status/…"}
          ]
        }
      ]
    }
  ]
}
```

Rules enforced by `pipeline/sweeps.py` at load and by the site build (fail closed):

- `disclaimer` must match the owner’s sentence exactly.
- Each `date` is unique, ISO `YYYY-MM-DD`, Africa/Johannesburg calendar day.
- `kind` is one of `news`, `official`, `civil-society`, `x-pulse`.
- `label`, when present, is one of the five MediaWatch labels.
- Every item has a non-empty `summary` and at least one `https` source URL (every fact has a reference).
- Each source URL’s host is on the reviewed host list (standing `site_content.SOURCES` hosts, plus a short extra list of already-used official and major news hosts, including `x.com` / `twitter.com`).
- An `x.com` / `twitter.com` profile or `/status/` URL is allowed only for a **publishable** handle (`official`, `police`, `media`, `public-figure`) listed on that item.
- Handle spelling is `[A-Za-z0-9_]{1,15}`. Roles other than the four publishable roles are refused.
- A handle or source may set `protected: true`. Protected-post links are refused, including X `/status/` URLs for a handle marked protected or in the known-protected list. A protected public-figure or media account may still be named if the reference is a fetched public byline or official source, not an unseen post.
- Our wording (titles, places, summaries — including day summaries — and source titles) must not state the exact age of anyone under 18. The check lives in `pipeline/minor_guard.py` (necessary but not sufficient for RFC-006 §8.4). It covers year, month, week and day counts in digits or words (a general word-number parser, not a list that stops at “twenty”), forms such as “17-year-old”, “age 12”, “12 y/o”, “12 y.o.” and “eighteen-month-old”, and a bare under-18 number next to child, teen, boy, girl, baby, toddler, learner or pupil wording or in an appositive or brackets (for example “child (15)”, “teen (15)”, “the child, 7,”). A school grade tied to a person (“Grade 12 learner”, “grade 10 pupil”, “learner in grade 11”) is also refused. Use “a child” or “a teenager”, whichever the source supports. Adult ages, age bands of 18 and over, and sentence lengths such as “7 years in prison” are allowed. An external source URL is not rewritten if the publisher’s slug itself contains an age: that exposure is theirs.
- The catalog text is scanned for identity-number, phone and email patterns, and for local paths.
- A day with no cited items must use the generic lean summary. On a day that has cited items, the day summary may only name incident detail that already appears in those sourced items, including a lowercase place after in/at/near/outside.
- Catalog text must not use operational wording. The check is a case-insensitive word-boundary pattern that targets private-Hub phrases (“the Hub”, “noted on the Hub”, “private Hub”) together with “notes”, “internal”, “operational note(s)” and “private draft page”. A bare “hub” is not enough, so “community hub” is allowed. “Internal affairs” and “internal investigation” are left alone.

The builder writes `assets/sweeps-data.js` as `window.CW_SWEEPS = …;` (deterministic JSON: sorted keys, `ensure_ascii=False`, stable day order newest first). The page script reads that object. There is no `fetch`, so the existing CSP does not need a `connect-src` exception. `form-action 'none'` is unchanged: the search is not a submitting form.

### How a new day is produced

1. The CrimeWatch daily sweep runs as it does today (official, civil-society, 24-hour news, X pulse of publishable accounts, farm-tile check).
2. It writes a **publishable day object** — not the operational note — that already follows the POPIA rule: masked wording, a fetched reference on every fact, only publishable handles named.
3. That object is appended to `data/sweeps.json` on this branch (or a follow-on BAU branch) and reviewed like any other content change.
4. `build_site.py` and `site_gate.py` refuse a bad catalog.
5. The page appears on crimewatch.grokforum.site only after review and a named GO to deploy that edition (or a later deploy that includes it). The nightly job still does not deploy.

This RFC does not grant a standing auto-merge or auto-deploy for new days. Each edition goes through review and a GO (owner decision, t726u item 6).

## User interface

- New nav item, **Daily sweeps**, at `/daily-sweeps/`, also linked from the home page.
- A labelled **day** `<select>` lists every catalog date, newest first. Choosing a day shows that edition (summary, items, handles, fact labels, source links).
- A **search dialog** for handles, implemented as a labelled, always-visible search landmark (not a modal, so the day list and results stay on screen). The field is `type="search"`, with a `<datalist>` of publishable handles present in the catalog. Matching is case-insensitive and accepts the handle with or without `@`.
- Search scope (owner decision, t726u item 6): an empty search shows the selected day; a non-empty search looks across all loaded days and groups hits by day.
- No third-party widgets. DOM nodes are created from validated fields (`textContent` / `setAttribute`), not by concatenating HTML from the JSON.
- The exact t704u disclaimer is visible on this page, and also in the site footer and on the method page.

## Accessibility

- `lang="en-ZA"`, skip-to-content link, heading order (`h1` then `h2` / `h3`).
- The day control and the handle field have visible `<label>` elements tied with `for`.
- The search landmark has an accessible name (“Search sweeps by handle”).
- Result counts and “no matches” are announced in an `aria-live="polite"` region.
- The selected day option and the current nav item use `aria-current` where it applies.
- Focus styles stay visible; keyboard-only use of the select and the search field is enough to operate the page. No information is carried by colour alone (fact labels also have text).
- `prefers-reduced-motion` is honoured (no added animation on this page).
